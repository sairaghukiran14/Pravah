Gate 1

Gate 2

Is the Google OAuth screen in production mode?

Google is your only sign-in method. If the consent screen is still in Testing — the default, and the single most common silent killer for indie NextAuth apps — then only email addresses you explicitly allowlisted can sign in at all. Everyone else hits a hard "Access blocked: Pravah has not completed the Google verification process" wall. Not a warning they can click past. A wall.

The check: Google Cloud Console → APIs & Services → OAuth consent screen. It must read In production. If it reads Testing, your signup count was mathematically zero no matter how good the post was.

Gate 3

Can a stranger see anything without an account?

Right now, no. The middleware gates /dashboard, /pipeline, /profile and /onboarding, and Google is the only way in. There is no step between "watched a 40-second video" and "hand over your Google account."

The fix that matters most: a public /demo route with the Coimbatore pipeline pre-loaded and no auth. Curiosity converts to interest there, and interest is what survives a signup form. Everything else on this page is worth a few percent; this one is worth the difference between zero and a number.

Your login page — the last screen before the decision — says "Welcome back" to a first-time stranger arriving from LinkedIn, and never mentions the ₹20 free credits your landing page advertises and your schema actually grants. The "Go to Dashboard" button under the Google button is a dead end that fires a Sign in required toast. Two small changes, made at the exact moment someone is deciding.

Why the link moved into the body

Your launch playbook says never put links in the post body. That rule is real, but post #1 applied it against its own goal. A body link costs you roughly 25% of reach. A comment link costs you roughly 80% of your clicks.

When the goal is impressions, the comment wins. When the goal is sign-ups, it is the wrong trade — and post #1 is the evidence. You bought 3,000 impressions with clicks you needed more.

Checked in the code

The ₹2.70 holds. From your rates in src/lib/api/pricing.ts — STT 0.375 + Translate (600 chars × 0.003) + Summarize 0.5 = 2.675 credits. And 1 credit = ₹1 is confirmed, not assumed: verify/route.ts increments the wallet by the rupee value of the Razorpay order. Quote the number with confidence.

The "seven runs": ₹20 free credits ÷ ₹2.675 = 7.5. Holds as long as the free grant in your schema stays at 20.

The URL: paste the real one and open it in incognito first. A dead link in the body is far worse than a dead link in a comment — it is the most visible line in the post.

Why this beats post #1's structure

  • One use case, not eleven. Post #1 listed five models, six pipelines and seven stack items. Nobody forwards a spec sheet. One story is repeatable — a reader can retell it to a colleague in a sentence.
  • A number nobody expects. ₹2.70 is the shareable atom. It survives being pulled out of context, which is exactly what happens when someone reshares.
  • Five hashtags, not eight. Eight reads as reach-farming and dilutes topical classification.
  • The honesty paragraph is a feature. Unqualified healthcare claims get torn apart in comments by actual clinicians. Pre-empting it converts your sharpest potential critic into your most credible commenter.
  • A question anyone can answer. "What do your users type that they'd rather say?" needs no technical knowledge — which widens the comment pool well past engineers, and comments are what drive reach.
  • It asks for the thing you actually want. Post #1 asked for opinions and received opinions. This one asks people to go use the product, names the price of trying (zero), and puts the link where it can be seen. You get what you ask for — so ask for the right thing.

The one number that matters

Sign-ups. Not impressions, not reactions. Post #1 already proved you can reach 3,000 people and change nothing — reach was never the missing ingredient.

If impressions drop to 2,200 and sign-ups go from 0 to 5, that is a large win, and it will feel like a loss on the LinkedIn analytics screen. Decide now that you'll read it correctly, because the vanity number is the one the platform puts in front of you.

Instrument it before you post

You cannot tune a funnel you can't see. Before launch, put a ?ref=li2 on the URL in the post body, and make sure you can answer three questions on the night: how many landed, how many reached the login screen, how many finished. Right now you know only the first and last, which is why post #1 gave you a zero and no explanation for it.
